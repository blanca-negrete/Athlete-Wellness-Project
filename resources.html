Wellness Resources

College student-athletes balance practices, competitions, academics, recovery, and personal responsibilities. Explore these resources for practical ideas to support your overall well-being.

Not sure where to start? Complete your wellness check-in to identify an area you may want to focus on.

Mental Wellness

Mental wellness is an important part of being a student-athlete. Making time to reflect and seek support can help you navigate challenges.

  • Take a few minutes to reflect on how you are feeling.
  • Talk with a trusted friend, coach, mentor, or counselor.
  • Make time for interests and activities outside your sport.
  • Reach out for professional support when you need it.

Physical Recovery

Recovery supports the balance between training, competition, rest, and everyday life. Individual recovery needs can vary.

  • Prioritize consistent sleep and rest.
  • Eat regular, balanced meals and stay hydrated.
  • Follow recovery guidance from your athletic training staff.
  • Tell a qualified healthcare professional about persistent pain or concerns about an injury.

Academic Balance

Managing coursework alongside practices and competitions can be challenging. A clear plan can help you stay organized.

  • Write down assignments, exams, practices, and deadlines.
  • Break larger assignments into smaller tasks.
  • Use gaps in your schedule for manageable schoolwork.
  • Contact instructors or academic support early when needed.

Stress Management

Stress can come from sport, academics, relationships, and other responsibilities. Building manageable routines may help you respond to stressful periods.

  • Divide your day into manageable priorities.
  • Try slow breathing or a short, quiet break.
  • Schedule time for rest and activities you enjoy.
  • Ask for help when responsibilities feel overwhelming.

Need Additional Support?

Athlete Wellness is an educational resource and is not a substitute for professional medical care, mental health counseling, or advice from qualified healthcare providers.

Consider contacting your campus counseling center, athletic training staff, healthcare provider, or another trusted support person if you need additional help.

If you are experiencing an immediate emergency, contact local emergency services.
